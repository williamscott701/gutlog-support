GutLog: IBS Food & Symptom Diary · iOS

GutLog is a food and symptom diary for IBS that works out which foods keep showing up before your symptoms, how strongly, and how long after eating. This page is where to get help with it.

Contact

Email williamscott701@gmail.com. GutLog is made by one person, so replies take as long as they take — but every message is read.

It helps to include your iOS version, your device, and what you were doing when something went wrong. Please don't send diary entries you'd rather keep private; they are never needed to diagnose a problem.

Do I need an account?

No. GutLog has no sign-up, no login, and no server. Everything you enter stays on your device. That also means there is no password to reset and no account for anyone to look up — if something is wrong, the answer is on your phone, not at our end.

How long before it finds anything?

About a week. GutLog needs roughly seven days of logging and fifteen meals before it will report its first finding, because below that the numbers aren't worth trusting. Consistency matters more than volume: a diary with every meal in it for ten days is far more useful than a patchy one kept for two months. Turning on the mealtime reminders in Settings is the single biggest thing you can do to get an answer sooner.

How does it decide something is a trigger?

For every food, FODMAP group and food characteristic you've logged, GutLog compares how often your symptoms followed it against how often they showed up without it, in three windows: within 4 hours, 4–12 hours later, and 12–48 hours later. The comparison is a chi-square test, and the strength label — emerging, likely or strong — comes from that result together with how many times you've eaten the food.

The strongest finding is accepted first, the symptom episodes it explains are set aside, and the rest is scored again. That is what stops a daily staple eaten at lunch from taking the blame for a reaction to dinner. It also means a finding can change or disappear as you log more, which is a feature: the app is showing you what your current diary supports, not a verdict.

A correlation is not a cause. GutLog is telling you where to look, and what to raise with a clinician or test in a proper reintroduction challenge.

Where is my data, and how do I get it out?

On your device, and nowhere else. GutLog has no networking code beyond the App Store's own billing, and no analytics of any kind.

Pro subscribers can export a doctor-ready PDF report and a CSV of every entry from the Report screen, and send them wherever they like through the normal iOS share sheet.

To erase everything, use Settings → Delete all data in the app, or delete the app itself. Because nothing is stored off-device, deleting the app deletes the diary with it. There is no copy left behind for anyone to remove, and no way to recover it afterwards, so export first if you want to keep it.

A food I eat isn't in the list

GutLog ships with 270 common foods with their FODMAP groups and trigger characteristics already tagged, which is a starting point rather than a world cuisine. Anything missing can be added with Add your own food in the food picker: give it a name, and tag its FODMAP groups and characteristics if you know them. Custom foods are analysed exactly like the built-in ones, and stay on your device with everything else.

If you don't know a food's FODMAP groups, leave them blank. The food is still tracked by name, and it can still be found as a trigger on its own.

The low-FODMAP protocol

The guided protocol has two phases. Elimination cuts the high-FODMAP groups for a few weeks and gives you an avoid-and-swap list for each one. Reintroduction then tests the six groups one at a time, three days each, with a dose schedule and a verdict at the end of each challenge.

It is a demanding diet and it is not meant to be permanent. The point of the reintroduction phase is to give you back everything you don't actually react to. Doing this alongside a dietitian is the recommended approach, and the exported report is designed to make that conversation shorter.

My widget isn't updating

Widgets refresh on a schedule iOS controls, so a small delay is normal. If a widget is stuck or shows nothing at all, remove it from the Home Screen or Lock Screen and add it again. Opening the app once afterwards gives it fresh data to read.

Reminders aren't arriving

Check that notifications are allowed for GutLog in the iOS Settings app, under Notifications → GutLog, and that reminders are switched on in GutLog's own Settings tab. Focus modes and Scheduled Summary can also hold notifications back. The Settings tab shows how many reminders are currently scheduled, which is the quickest way to tell whether the app or the system is the problem.

GutLog Pro, trials, and cancelling

Logging is free forever, with no limits on how much you record. GutLog Pro is an optional subscription that unlocks the trigger analysis, safe foods, the lifestyle correlations, the guided reintroduction phase, history beyond the last 30 days, and the PDF and CSV export. It is sold monthly or yearly, and the yearly plan includes a one-week free trial for new subscribers.

Prices are set in your own App Store storefront and shown in your local currency, including any tax Apple collects for your country. Subscriptions are billed through your Apple Account and renew automatically unless auto-renew is switched off at least 24 hours before the period ends. You can manage or cancel at any time, including during a free trial, in Settings → your name → Subscriptions on your device. Cancelling is handled entirely by Apple — we can't do it for you, and we never see your payment details.

Already subscribed but the app doesn't know it — on a new phone, for instance? Tap Restore purchases on the Pro screen, signed in with the same Apple Account you bought it with.

Refunds are handled by Apple, not by us, at reportaproblem.apple.com.

GutLog is not a medical device

GutLog records what you tell it and shows you the patterns in it. It does not diagnose anything, it does not recommend treatment, and it is not a substitute for care from a doctor or a dietitian who knows your situation. A pattern in your diary is a starting point for that conversation, not a conclusion.

Some symptoms need a doctor, not a diary. Blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, a fever, persistent vomiting, difficulty swallowing, anaemia, symptoms that wake you at night, or a first onset of symptoms after the age of about 50 are not things to track and watch — please have them looked at. If you think you may be having a medical emergency, contact your local emergency services right away.
