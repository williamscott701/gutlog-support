1. The short version

GutLog doesn't collect your data, because GutLog doesn't collect data. Everything you enter — your meals, your symptoms, your bowel movements, your daily factors, your notes, any photo you attach — is written into GutLog's own private storage area on your iPhone or iPad: the same sandboxed, on-device storage most apps use, protected by your device's passcode, Face ID or Touch ID like everything else on your phone. There is no account for it to be attached to, and no server for it to travel to. If you delete GutLog, that data is deleted with it. Nobody at William Scott can see what you enter, because it never reaches us in the first place.

The one thing on your device that ever talks to a server is the App Store's own billing system, when you subscribe to GutLog Plus — that is covered in "Subscriptions and payment" below.

2. Health information, and what GutLog is not

A food and symptom diary is, by its nature, a record of sensitive health information about you. GutLog treats it accordingly: it stays on your device, it is never transmitted, and it is never analysed anywhere but on your own phone. The trigger analysis, the safe-food list and the lifestyle correlations are all computed on-device, in the app, from your own entries.

GutLog is not a medical device. It records what you tell it and shows you the patterns in it. It does not diagnose anything, does not recommend treatment, and is not a substitute for care from a doctor or dietitian who knows your situation. A correlation in your diary is a place to start a conversation, not a conclusion. What is true of people in general may not be true of you.

Some symptoms need a doctor rather than a diary — among them blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, fever, persistent vomiting, difficulty swallowing, anaemia, symptoms that wake you at night, or a first onset of symptoms later in life. If you think you may be having a medical emergency, contact your local emergency services right away.

3. No accounts, no sign-in

GutLog doesn't ask you to sign up, sign in, or create a profile. There is no username, no password, and no email address collected by the app, and no server-side account of any kind for your information to attach to. Nothing you do in GutLog is tied to your identity anywhere but on your own device.

The app does ask, optionally, for a first name during setup. It is used for one thing: printing a name on the PDF report you might hand to a clinician. You can leave it blank, change it at any time under You, or switch it off for the report entirely.

4. What GutLog stores, and where

Everything below is stored locally, using Apple's on-device SwiftData framework, inside GutLog's own private storage area on your device. None of it is transmitted to us, to Apple, or to anyone else — GutLog has no networking code that could send it anywhere, aside from the App Store billing system described later in this policy.

4.1 Meals

The date and time, the meal type, the foods you picked (from the built-in catalogue or ones you added yourself), a portion size, an optional free-text note, and an optional photo.

4.2 Symptoms

The date and time, which symptom it was, a severity from 1 to 10, its duration if you enter one, and an optional free-text note.

4.3 Bowel movements

The date and time, the Bristol stool scale type, urgency, whether the movement felt complete, whether you noted blood or mucus, and an optional free-text note. The blood and mucus flags exist because they matter clinically and belong in a report you show a doctor.

4.4 Daily factors

One record per day: a stress rating, hours and quality of sleep, minutes of exercise, glasses of water, the names of any medications or supplements you choose to enter, whether you are menstruating if you choose to record it, and an optional note.

4.5 Your own foods

Any food you add yourself: its name, and the FODMAP groups and characteristics you tag it with.

4.6 The low-FODMAP protocol

Which phase you are in and when it started, and for each reintroduction challenge, the group being tested, the dates, the doses, the symptoms you record during it, and the verdict.

4.7 Your app settings

A handful of small preferences — whether you've finished setup, your optional name, IBS subtype and goal, your reminder times and on/off choices, your analysis threshold, and the foods you've logged most recently — stored using iOS's standard on-device settings storage, the same mechanism nearly every app uses for simple preferences. These stay on your device too.

Free-text notes are exactly that: genuinely blank until you type into them, with nothing read, scanned, or analysed by anything, and stored as you wrote them.

5. Photos

You can attach a photo to a meal. When you do, iOS shows you its own photo picker; GutLog is given only the single image you choose and never gets access to the rest of your photo library. The image is compressed and stored inside the app's private storage alongside the meal, on your device. It is not uploaded anywhere, and no image is read, scanned, or analysed by GutLog or by anyone else. Deleting the meal, or removing the photo from it, deletes the copy GutLog holds.

6. Widgets

If you add a GutLog widget to your Home Screen or Lock Screen, the app writes a small summary — what you've logged today, your logging streak, and your top trigger if you have one — into a storage area shared between the GutLog app and its widget. Both run on your own device. This exists purely so the widget, which runs as its own lightweight process without access to the app's full database, can show something useful without opening the app. That shared summary never leaves your device: it is not a sync feature, and there is no version of it that reaches a server, iCloud, or another device you own.

7. Notifications

GutLog's reminders are local notifications, scheduled by the app on your device and delivered by iOS. They are not push notifications: nothing is sent from a server, and no device token, identifier or notification content ever leaves your phone. If you decline the notification permission, or turn reminders off later, the app simply schedules nothing. The reminder text never contains anything you have logged.

8. Subscriptions and payment

GutLog Plus is sold as an auto-renewing subscription through Apple's App Store, using Apple's own in-app purchase system. That transaction happens entirely between you and Apple.

We never see, receive, or store your name, your payment card, your billing address, or your Apple Account details. What the app receives from Apple's StoreKit framework is a signed receipt saying whether this device's Apple Account currently has an active subscription, and when it renews or expires. GutLog reads that to decide whether to unlock the Plus features, and it is not linked to anything you have logged.

Apple's handling of the purchase is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy, not by this one. Apple provides the developer with anonymised, aggregated sales reports — how many subscriptions were sold in a country, for instance — which contain no information about individual customers and cannot be connected to any person or to any diary.

9. Exporting and sharing your data

GutLog Plus subscribers can generate a PDF report and a CSV file containing their diary. Both are created on your device. Where they go afterwards is entirely your decision: the iOS share sheet hands the file to whatever you pick — Mail, Messages, Files, AirDrop, a printer, another app. Once you send it somewhere, that destination's own privacy terms apply to the copy you sent, and it is outside our reach. Nothing is exported automatically, and nothing is ever sent to us.

10. Device backups

If you back up your device to iCloud or to a computer, your GutLog data is included in that backup like any other app's. Those backups are Apple's, governed by Apple's terms and encryption, and they exist so that your diary survives a lost phone. We have no access to them, and no ability to read, restore, or delete one. If you would rather your diary were not in a backup, iOS lets you exclude individual apps from iCloud Backup in its own Settings.

11. No tracking, analytics, or advertising

GutLog contains no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting service, no advertising, no advertising identifier, and no third-party code that reports on how you use the app. Nothing tracks you across apps or websites, so the App Tracking Transparency prompt never appears — there is nothing for it to ask about. The app's Privacy Manifest, which Apple requires and publishes on the App Store product page as "Data Not Collected", says the same thing in Apple's own format.

12. Deleting your data

Two ways, both immediate and both entirely in your hands. You → Delete all data inside the app erases every entry while keeping the app installed. Deleting the app from your device removes the app and its storage together.

Because nothing is stored off-device, there is no copy anywhere for us to delete on your behalf, and no way for us or anyone else to recover it once it is gone. Export what you want to keep before you delete.

13. Your rights, wherever you are

GutLog is available worldwide, and privacy laws differ from country to country. Rights such as access, correction, deletion, portability and objection — under the EU and UK GDPR, the California Consumer Privacy Act, Brazil's LGPD, India's DPDP Act, and comparable laws elsewhere — all concern personal data held by a company about you.

In GutLog's case there is nothing for those requests to reach: we hold no personal data about you at all. In practice you already exercise every one of those rights directly, without asking anyone. Access and portability are the report and CSV export; correction is editing an entry; deletion is the button under You or deleting the app. There is no profiling, no automated decision-making with legal effects, and no sale or sharing of personal information — we have none to sell. If you would like this confirmed in writing for your own records, email us and we will confirm it.

14. Children's privacy

GutLog is intended for adults managing their own digestive health, and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone, of any age — there is no mechanism in the app by which we could. A parent or guardian who wants a child's diary removed can delete it from the device directly, using either method in section 12.

15. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new "last updated" date. Because the app collects nothing, any change is likely to be a clarification rather than a new use of your data. If that ever stopped being true — if a future version of GutLog needed to send something off your device — it would be described here plainly, before it shipped, and it would be something you opt into.
